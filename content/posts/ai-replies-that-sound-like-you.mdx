If you run a small business, I'd bet most of your emails are versions of the same handful of questions. Do you cover my area? How much is it? When can you come? Can I change my booking?

Answering them isn't hard. It's just slow, and it eats the bit of the day you meant to spend on actual work. AI tools like ChatGPT are good at this kind of job. The catch is that the default output sounds like nobody you've ever met.

So here's how I'd set it up to sound like you.

## Collect ten replies you've already sent

Go through your sent folder and copy out ten replies you were happy with. Real ones. Remove names, addresses, phone numbers and anything else that identifies a customer before you paste them anywhere.

Those ten replies teach the tool more about how you write than any description of your "tone" ever will.

## Write a short brief about your business

Then write a few lines about your business, as if you were explaining it to someone covering for you while you're on holiday. What you do, where you work, roughly what things cost, and anything you always or never say.

Something like this:

```
I run a window cleaning business in Leeds and the surrounding villages.
Regular cleans are every 4 or 8 weeks. Prices start at £15 for a small terrace.
I write short, friendly replies and sign off with "Cheers, Dan".
I never promise a time slot by email. I always say I'll text the day before.
```

## Ask the AI tool to draft a reply

Paste the brief and the example replies into the tool, then add the customer's question with their details removed. Ask it to draft a reply in the same style as the examples.

Most tools let you save this as a reusable set of instructions, so you only do the setup once. In ChatGPT these are called custom instructions or a custom GPT. Others call them projects.

## Check every draft before you send it

I wouldn't ever let a tool send replies on its own. It will occasionally make something up, like a price you don't charge or a day you don't work. Read every draft, fix what's wrong and then send it. It's still much faster than starting from a blank email.

## Customer details you should never paste in

Be careful with customer details. Under UK data protection law you're responsible for what happens to your customers' personal information, and pasting it into a third-party tool is sharing it. Strip out names, addresses, phone numbers and anything sensitive first. If the question only makes sense with those details, answer that one yourself.

Done properly, this turns a twenty-minute inbox job into five. And the replies still sound like they came from you, because they mostly did.
