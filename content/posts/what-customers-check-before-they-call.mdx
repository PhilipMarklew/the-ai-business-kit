Think about the last time you needed a plumber, a printer or a dog groomer you'd never used. You probably found three or four options, opened their websites, and ruled most of them out before reading a single full sentence.

That's what your customers do to you. So what are they actually looking at?

## Can I tell you're real?

This is the first filter, and it's the one small businesses miss most often. A customer wants to know there's an actual person or team behind the site.

The fixes are simple. Put a real phone number where people can see it. Add your town or the area you cover. Use a photo of you, your van, your shop or your work, not a stock photo of people in a meeting room. Stock photos make a site feel like a template, and people notice.

## Is anyone saying good things?

Reviews do more work than anything you write about yourself. But a page of five reviews from 2021 can hurt more than it helps, because it suggests things went quiet.

If you have recent reviews on Google, link to them. If a customer sends a nice email, ask if you can quote it. Real first names and a town are enough.

## Do I know roughly what it costs?

I think this is the one owners resist most. You might not be able to give exact prices, and that's fine. But "from £80" or "most jobs fall between £150 and £300" tells someone whether to keep reading.

Hiding prices completely makes people assume you're expensive. Or that you'll make it up on the spot.

## Will anything break when I try to get in touch?

Try it yourself, on your phone. Tap the phone number. Does it ring? Fill in your own contact form. Does the email arrive? How long until you'd normally reply?

A broken form doesn't just lose that one enquiry. It's invisible. You'd never know it happened.

## Does it look looked after?

Small things add up. A footer that still says 2022. A blog with one post from three years ago. A padlock warning in the browser bar because the site isn't on HTTPS. None of these are disasters on their own, but together they say nobody's minding the shop.

Pick one of these this week and fix it. The phone number and the contact form test take ten minutes, and they're the ones most likely to be costing you work right now.
